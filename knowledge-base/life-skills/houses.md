# Houses

Houses in Black Desert: buying and selling them, the functions a house can be set to, placing furniture and how to obtain furniture.

## Houses overview

A house is an individual space apart from the common game areas, and each Adventurer can own every house in the world if they have the silver and contribution points, even hundreds. The number usable as residences is limited. Houses are mostly used for life skill production, but their interiors can be decorated with wallpapers and furniture, and they are places to play with pets, socialize with friends and guild members, rest, repair equipment or read books.

## Houses buying and selling

Open the World Map (hotkey M) and click a town to see the houses for sale; purchasable ones are blue. Buying a house makes the adjacent houses available, so to get a gray house you must first buy neighbors. A house costs contribution points; larger and more functional houses cost more, so consider a house's strategic value first.

A house can be sold at any time to recover the invested contribution points, but the time and silver spent improving the house and changing its function are not refunded. The Contribution page of the wiki covers contribution points.

## Houses usages and changing them

Each house needs a usage chosen at purchase; if none is selected it becomes storage by default. A usage can be changed at any time, but this takes time and silver and the house cannot be used meanwhile. A house can be a personal residence, worker lodging, town storage, refinery, workshop, workbench or textile mill.

## Houses as residences

A residence holds production equipment such as alchemy and cooking tools, and furniture. You can initially set up to 5 houses as residences, regardless of territory, and this can be expanded later. The Residence icon at the top left of the screen opens My Residence List, whose Location button shows the travel route to a residence. Changing a residence to another usage automatically moves its furniture to the town storage.

## Houses as worker lodging

Lodgings are where hired workers live in a given town. Your first worker in a town needs no lodging, but more workers there need it. Workers gather materials at production nodes or craft at workshops, and their results go to the town storage. When crafting or doing activities in a house, workers use the ingredients in the storage of their own home town. The Workers and Crafting pages of the wiki cover this further.

## Houses as storage, refinery and horse ranch

Storage: a house set as storage adds slots to the town storage, and upgrading its level adds more slots.

Refinery: crafts black stone powder, a common base ingredient for most workshop items, and with the right materials workers can also craft black stones.

Horse ranch: available only in major cities and towns; it raises the number of mount slots at the stable, where mounts are checked in.

## Houses as workshops and shipyards

A worker sent to a workshop crafts items, and upgrading the workshop level allows better items. Some workshop types:

| Workshop | Crafts |
| Weapon Workshop | Sharp-edge weapons such as long swords and axes |
| Armor Workshop | Helmets, gloves, shoes and other defense gear |
| Carpentry Workshop | Wooden bows and shields |
| Handcraft Workshop | Amulets, Ornamental Knots, Talismans and similar |
| Tool Workshop | All gathering tools |
| Cannon Workshop | Cannons and cannonballs for Siege War |
| Furniture Workshop | Furniture for residences |
| Jeweler | All accessories |
| Horse Gear Workshop | All horse equipment |

Other workshop types exist depending on the town. A workshop for materials processes gathered goods into trade goods or crafting materials, with specialized factories for fish, mushrooms, crops, wood and ores. A shipyard and wagon workshop crafts a wagon or ship, giving a registration emblem to exchange for the vehicle with an NPC at a wharf or stable.

## Houses furniture types and effects

In a residence you can place functional furniture such as alchemy tools and cooking utensils and decorative pieces. A bed restores energy when you rest, bookshelves let you read for knowledge, and some handmade pieces give buffs when interacted with. Objects are classified into six placement types:
- Floor Decor: beds, sofas, tables, desks, chairs, bookcases, tools and most objects.
- Flooring: changes the floor look; one-time use, not restorable once removed or replaced.
- Wall Decor: curtains, paintings, seasonal decorations, hunting trophies.
- Wallpaper: covers all walls; one-time use.
- On the Table / Floor: small items on top of larger objects, such as dishes, flowerpots, candle stands.
- Ceiling Decor: items like chandeliers.

Selling a residence or changing its function permanently destroys its flooring and wallpaper.

## Houses placing furniture step by step

Enter a purchased residence by pressing R on the door, then click Place Mode at the top of the screen. Select furniture on the right and move the mouse to the spot. Rotate with the [ or ] keys, or snap the angle to 45 degrees. The list includes owned furniture and Pearl Shop furniture, which can be previewed before buying. Press Space or double-click to place it. Unowned paid items show a BUY icon and join the Cart on the right; click Purchase All to buy. Esc exits Place Mode.

For wallpaper and flooring, select one in Place Mode and drag it onto the wall to preview, then press Space. In a two-story house one wallpaper covers both floors. Wallpaper and flooring purchases cannot be undone, and replacing wallpaper means deleting the old one or overlaying the new one.

## Houses interior points and visiting

Placing furniture and small objects earns interior points. A residence ranked 1st to 15th in points can be visited by other Adventurers: move to the house, press F5, then click Enter. A player ranked 1st in a town can use that town's investment banks, where available.

## Houses how to acquire furniture

Furniture comes from many sources. Furniture Dealer NPCs in major towns include art dealers, carpet dealers and candlestick vendors; cheap but decent pieces often cost amity. Furniture Workshops let a worker craft furniture, which can give buffs. Other sources:
- Fishing: small decorative objects, at a low chance.
- Stealing: from some NPCs, with a slim chance.
- Monster drops: a very low chance, mostly unusual shapes with combat buffs.
- Quest rewards from some NPCs.
- Hunting trophies: dropped by hunted animals, with rarer ones crafted from hunting materials.
- Pearl Shop: unique sets; first check whether they can be obtained in the game, since some towns let you craft the same items.

Higher-end furniture needs high amity with certain NPCs.
