# Fishing

Beginner guide to the Fishing life skill: rods and floats, where to fish, the fishing minigame, auto-fishing, fish expiry, and the Fish Guide.

## Fishing: rods, floats and where to fish

Your character has a basic fishing rod equipped by default, so you do not need to buy one. Better rods let you fish longer and catch rarer fish. Floats attached to a rod raise the chance of a particular type of fish, such as bigger or rarer ones. Rods and floats come from quest rewards, town workshop crafting, or the Central Market.

You can fish wherever there is water: swamps, creeks, lakes, rivers, coastlines, and open sea. You can also fish from rowboats, fishing boats, and larger boats, but not in water that is too shallow.

When a rod is equipped, fishing UI elements appear at the top of the screen. They show the area's fishing resource state and offer options to avoid being carried away on other Adventurers' ships and to choose which fish grades to discard. Turning on the ship option is recommended.

## Fishing: discarding fish by grade

You can choose which fish grades you do not want to receive. The grades you can discard depend on your fishing level:

- Below Professional 1: white items only.
- Professional 1 onward: green grade items too.
- Artisan 1 onward: blue grade items too.
- Master 1 onward: yellow grade items too.

This is useful during auto-fishing to avoid filling your inventory with low grade fish.

## Fishing resources in an area

When many Adventurers fish in the same place, the area's fishing resources drop and fishing takes longer. The states are Abundant, Average, Not enough, and Exhausted. You can see the state at the top of the screen when a rod is equipped, just above the fishing filters, or on the World Map (hotkey: M) in the Territory Resource Information tab.

## Fishing steps and minigame

Swap from combat gear to your fishing rod by clicking the icon in the icon bar, or hold the ' key to open the ring menu that switches between combat gear, fishing rod, and harpoon.

With the rod equipped, stand near water and press Space to cast. Wait for a bite, which takes longer or shorter depending on the area's resources. When a fish bites, press Space again to start the minigame.

In the minigame a moving gauge appears and you get three chances to press Space to stop it in the right spot. After that you must press the WASD keys in the order shown within a time limit. The command difficulty depends on the fish's category, and success is not guaranteed. Fishing earns fishing EXP, and a higher fishing level improves your chances of better-grade fish. The fish you can catch differs by territory.

## Fishing: energy gauge

You can raise your chances of higher grade fish by spending energy on the fishing rod. Hold the Spacebar to charge the gauge with up to 10 energy points. The more energy you spend, the better your chance of catching additional fish. The rod icon fills with a red indicator as you charge.

## Fishing: auto-fishing

If a fish is hooked and you do not press Space to begin the minigame, auto-fishing starts by itself. After a set time you get the item without playing the minigame. The wait depends on your rod, the area's fishing resources, and the stackable auto-fishing time reductions from dishes, elixirs, pet skills, and similar sources. You can set which fish grades to keep, and you can tick the option to not board other adventurers' ships.

## Fishing: fish expiry, drying and trade use

Fish are used for cooking and alchemy, and rare ones can be expensive trade items. Most fish carry a price guarantee timer. As time passes, a fish loses value, and when the timer ends the fish is expired and unusable. An expired fish has no use: you can discard it, or sell it but receive nothing.

To keep fish for cooking, dry them through processing (hotkey: L). Dried fish ignore the price guarantee but lose their trade item status. Dried fish can become a trade item again if you box it up at a town workshop.

## Fishing: Fish Guide

The Fish Guide shows only fish you have caught yourself. For each one it lists the knowledge entry, how many you have caught, your largest size, and a leaderboard of the biggest fish of each species. Sizes are measured to three decimal places. It also shows how your size ranks against the species average, and has progress by category: Freshwater, Saltwater, Crustacean, and Other.

The guide updates when you beat your record, but if you log off right after the catch your data might not be saved. On the leaderboard, ties go to whoever caught that size first. When you catch a new record fish an icon appears, and pressing it opens the New Max Size window.

Open the guide from the main menu (hotkey: Esc), then Life, then Fish Guide. For deeper detail the wiki points to its Advanced Fishing Guide page.
