# Cooking

Guide to the Cooking life skill: utensils, ingredients and substitutes, how to cook, success and failure, starter recipes, selling dishes, mastery, ladles, and quests.

## Cooking: what you need to start

Cooking makes dishes that give buffs useful in all kinds of content, and dishes sell to Adventurers and NPCs for steady income. To start you need three things: cooking utensils installed in your residence, fresh ingredients, and sometimes a seasoning bought from an NPC shop. Cook's clothes that shorten cooking time are one of the best early investments.

The utensils can only be installed in a residence. It is handy to have a residence near the town storage holding your ingredients, and a cheap residence costing 1 contribution point is recommended for that. You can own several residences.

## Cooking utensils and how to get them

Simple, less durable utensils are sold by NPC shops such as an innkeeper or food vendor. They have no special effect and last only around 100 uses, which is fine for small quick batches. Durable intermediate and advanced utensils are crafted at a town workshop.

- Intermediate cooking utensil: durability 500. Materials: black stone powder x7, melted iron shards x15, logs x10, rough stones x25.
- Advanced cooking utensil: at least 900 cooks and cooking time reduced by 1 second. It is crafted at level 3 tool workshops or bought on the Central Market. Materials: black stone powder x14, melted iron shards x20, usable scantlings x5, polished stones x20. It is the recommended utensil until you can get a supreme one.
- Regional traditional utensils last longer than intermediate or advanced ones, but their special effect makes cooking take longer.
- Morning Light Traditional Cooking Utensil: -1 cooking time like the advanced one, with much more durability. It can be crafted in two houses, Morning Light Nopsae 4 and Morning Light Nampo 2, from fire horn x40, iron ingot x20, standardized timber square x20, and polished stone x80.

## Cooking ingredients and cook's clothes

Cooking ingredients come in two kinds: seasonings such as sugar and salt from any cooking vendor, and ingredients like apples and pork that you gather, process, or cultivate. Workers sent to nodes can collect many ingredients, which ties into managing contribution points. A stable ingredient supply is key. The crafting notes on the Adventurer's Board (hotkey: F1 or F2) show where to find each ingredient.

Silver embroidered cook's clothes improve movement speed and cut cooking time by 1 second in their base form, and gain cooking experience bonuses as you enhance them. A costume mill town workshop can craft them from black stone powder x15, powder of earth x10, and cotton fabrics x9. At maximum enhancement they give +4 movement speed, +40% cooking experience, and 7 seconds less cooking time.

## Cooking process and batch production

Bring the ingredients to your residence and place utensils with Place Mode, which appears when you enter. Stand in front of the utensil and press R or click the Controls button to open the Start Cooking interface. The window lists every dish in the game. Dishes you have cooked at least once show their exact name, and clicking one shows the recipe you used.

Right-click ingredients in your inventory to add them. Nothing is lost until you press Start Cooking. A dish usually needs 1 to 3 ingredients and 1 to 3 seasonings, and some need finished dishes as ingredients.

For repeats, put in the ingredients for a single cook and click Batch Production. The default quantity is one, so press F in the number window or click Maximum Quantity. Even if you load all the ingredients into the slots, Batch Production only makes one dish from them, so load just one cook's worth.

## Cooking substitutes

A substitute is another ingredient of the same category that can replace a required one. For example, any grain can replace wheat. Some dishes need more of the substitute than the original, so a failed cook may mean you need more. Some dishes, such as gukbap, accept no substitutes.

## Cooking success and failure

If you prepare the ingredients exactly as the recipe says, you never fail, even with extra ingredients. Your first success gives the dish's knowledge entry, recorded in the Knowledge interface (hotkey: H). Some dishes require a specific cooking level, so a low level fails even with the right recipe and ingredients.

Other causes of failure are wrong ingredients (100% failure) or too few (sometimes works, depending on the ratio). A higher cooking level gives better quality dishes and larger quantities.

A successful cook may also give a witch's delicacy. An NPC trades it for cooking and contribution experience, milk, or beer. Milk can only be obtained by hand from cows, and beer keeps workers motivated. Store these and exchange them all at once.

## Cooking: starter recipes (vinegar, beer, essence of liquor)

Recipes (Golden Ratio) to level up early:

- Vinegar (cooking beginner 1 or higher): 1 Grain, 1 Fruit, 1 Leavening Agent, 1 Sugar. Grain can be wheat, barley, potato, sweet potato, or corn. Food vendors sell leavening agent and sugar. Vinegar is an ingredient in many dishes, so save it.
- Beer (beginner 1 or higher; cool draft beer at skilled 1 and higher): 5 Grain, 6 Mineral Water, 2 Leavening Agent, 1 Sugar. For garden-grown grain, high-quality grain counts as 2 and special grain as 1, so 2 high-quality wheat or 1 special wheat replaces 5 wheat. Beer restores worker stamina.
- Essence of Liquor (beginner 1 or higher): 1 Flour, 1 Fruit, 1 Leavening Agent. Flour is made by grinding grain through processing (hotkey: L). Grapes and strawberries are recommended fruit. It is used in citron cider, coconut cocktail, and fruit wine.

## Cooking: selling dishes

Because cooking buffs are useful everywhere, selling dishes earns money, but you must follow trends. Beer sells constantly on the Central Market but its low price makes little profit. Regional meals such as Balenos meal are always in demand. Compare options: selling beer, using it in complex dishes, selling to Adventurers, or packing boxes for the Imperial Crafting Delivery NPCs to deliver to the imperial court.

## Cooking: best equipment for efficiency

- Silver embroidered cook's clothes (see the enhancement table).
- Advanced utensil: durability 900, -1 second cooking time.
- Supreme cooking utensil: turn in cooking boxes to Imperial Crafting Delivery NPCs for silver and golden seals. 500 golden seals buy a supreme utensil, which also reduces cooking time by 1 second and has 5,000 durability.
- Alchemy Stone of Life or Life Spirit Stone: even the worst reduces cooking time by half a second and stacks with all other reductions. Make via alchemy or buy on the Central Market. Some monsters drop life spirit stones.
- Dishes and draughts such as seafood cron meal and verdure draught cut cooking time, but they cost more, so check the profit.

## Cooking mastery

Cooking mastery raises the maximum dishes per cook, extra dishes from the process, the chance of higher quality dishes, and profit from cooking boxes at Imperial Crafting Delivery NPCs. It also raises the chance to trigger mass cooking, which uses 10 times the ingredients to make 10 portions at once. Mass cooking does not cancel the max-dishes or extra-dishes effects. For box profit only, swap to mastery gear while turning in boxes.

## Cooking Ladle for mastery

Equip a Cooking Ladle to raise Cooking Mastery:

- Loggia Ladle: buy from NPC Camellia Loggia or Zaaira.
- Roroju Ladle: buy from NPC Geranoa or Zaaira.
- Manos Ladle: manufacture with Iron Ingot x10, Manos Stone x5, Magical Shard x50, Fundamentals of Cooking x10. Fundamentals of Cooking come from the repeatable quests [Repeat] Edman's, Heidi's, Izaro's, and Valks' Fundamentals of Cooking.

## Cooking quests and bonus quests

Many NPCs give cooking quests. The wiki suggests those in Balenos, especially Velia. Leap quests speed up leveling, and the first unlocks at cooking beginner 10: summon the Black Spirit and open its guide menu to be guided to the NPC. Rewards include contribution EXP, cooking EXP, and hard and sharp black crystal shards.
