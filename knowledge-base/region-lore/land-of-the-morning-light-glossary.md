# Land of the Morning Light Glossary

A glossary of Korean culture, folklore and history terms used in the Land of the Morning Light region of Black Desert.

## Land of the Morning Light glossary: provinces and officials

In the Land of the Morning Light, Donghae (the East Sea) names the eastern province and Hwanghae (the Yellow Sea) names the western province. Taebaek Mountain in the game presides over Donghae. The Yeongam of the East, also spelled Youngam, is the highest authority in Donghae, serving as warden and governor with the military rank of Lieutenant General. Satto is the title commoners use for the top local official, such as the hyungam of Dalbeol or the yeongam of the east. A hyungam is a local governor of the smallest district, as in Dalbeol Village. A yibang headed one of six local government departments. Yangban were the Korean nobility, and seonbi were scholars without government posts.

## Land of the Morning Light glossary: spirits and creatures

In the Land of the Morning Light glossary, Oduksini is a dark folktale monster that grows larger the longer people watch it, and in Black Desert it is the name for the black spirits. Dokkebi are folklore beings who sometimes trick humans and sometimes help them; unlike ghosts, they form when an inanimate object is spiritually possessed. The region's dokkebi call all humans Mr. Kim, because Kim is the most common Korean surname. Yogwe are legendary creatures, equivalent to Japanese yokai; the Gumiho and Imoogi count as yogwe, and a group of bandits in the Land follows the Gumiho. A mudang is a shaman, and jangseung are wooden totem poles marking village borders.

## Land of the Morning Light glossary: everyday life

In the Land of the Morning Light glossary, an Auntie is a woman who sells alcohol or runs an inn, the equivalent of an innkeeper in the Republic of Calpheon. Gukbap is a hot rice soup, and a jangdok is an earthenware jar for pastes and preserved foods. Gisaeng were female entertainers at banquets. The danso is a bamboo flute, the janggu a drum, and the jing and kkwaenggwari are brass gongs. Baduk is the board game Go, and jegichagi is a game of kicking a paper-wrapped coin called a jegi. Jihwa-ja! is a shout used when singing, dancing or toasting.
