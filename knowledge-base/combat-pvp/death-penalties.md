# Death Penalties

What you lose when a character dies in Black Desert Online, how resurrection works, and the extra penalties from negative karma, deserts, jail and negative naval fame.

## Death penalties: what you lose when killed by monsters

The death penalty depends on what killed the character. Dying to monsters can reduce combat EXP, destroy magic crystals and destroy trade goods. Magic crystals break easily, so take care. The combat EXP loss becomes a real burden at higher levels.

Exceptions: a death in PvP gives no death penalty unless the character involved has negative karma. Deaths to World Bosses, Field Bosses and Atoraxxion also carry no death penalty.

## Death penalties: resurrection spot choices

After dying you choose where to resurrect. If you pick nothing within the 10-minute waiting time, the character is resurrected automatically depending on the situation. Reviving with an item such as Elion's Tear restores your combat EXP, but not destroyed magic crystals or trade goods.

- Instant Resurrection: revive on the exact spot with an Elion's Tear. In the arena you can revive on the spot without the item. The item cannot be used if you die in a conquest area or in the water.
- Resurrect at the Closest Town: you return to the town nearest to the node where you died. If no town in that territory is discovered, the closest possible town is used.
- Resurrect at the Closest Safe Zone: you return to the nearest already discovered node within the territory where you died. If no node is within a certain distance, this option may not be offered.
- Conquest War Resurrection: shown only to Conquest War participants, it revives you near the stronghold or command post you belong to.

## Death penalties: getting Elion's Tear

Elion's Tears can come from events, certain challenges and season content. You can also craft one through simple alchemy (hotkey: L) using Mystical Spirit Powder x100 and Tears of War x1. An Elion's Tear is the item for instant resurrection on the spot and for restoring combat EXP.

## Death penalties: negative karma and how karma drops

Family karma goes down when you harm others, such as stealing from NPCs or attacking other Adventurers without cause, and goes up when you kill monsters. If you counterattack and kill someone who flagged for PvP and hit you first, that counts as self-defense and does not change your karma. Otherwise it counts as player-killing.

If you start a fight by activating PvP mode yourself, your karma falls by 10,000 points. Killing the target costs another 200,000, so killing someone who never fought back costs 210,000 in total. Karma cannot go above 300,000, so killing more than 2 Adventurers in a row pushes you below 0 and brings heavy penalties. Below 0, your characters have negative karma.

## Death penalties: negative karma consequences

A negative karma character faces these extra penalties:

- Attacked in a Safe Zone: they can be attacked even in towns and suffer the death penalty if they die.
- Enhanced Death Penalty: more equipment magic crystals break, or enhancement levels drop. Crystal destruction and enhancement loss do not both happen on the same death, and enhancement loss cannot happen in the Valencian desert.
- Resurrection spot: choosing a node revives you at one of the three nearest nodes at random, whatever the territory or exploration status.
- Attacked by NPCs: town guards attack negative karma characters on sight and patrol borders, making normal town functions hard to use. Guards in the bandit haven of Muiquun do not attack you.

## Death penalties: desert fugitive status

In desert areas, such as the Black Desert, killing another Adventurer makes you a fugitive for 30 minutes even if your karma is not negative, with penalties similar to negative karma. The desert is lawless, so you can be attacked there by others even without PvP mode and with karma 0 or higher. Dying to a monster or the environment only breaks magic crystals and reduces combat EXP.

The fugitive mark is visible to everyone nearby. It disappears after 30 minutes or when you reach the jail, but dying in a non-desert area does not remove it. When you leave the desert, the disadvantages for your karma apply, so check that karma has not dropped below 0.

## Death penalties: jail and punishment debuff

A negative karma Adventurer killed by other Adventurers or NPCs in the desert is forcibly sent to jail and gets a punishment debuff. While it lasts, combat actions are limited, movement speed falls by 50%, mounts cannot be summoned and the main menu escape option will not take you out of jail. If you are killed in jail you resurrect there with no further penalty. You are also barred from Red Battlefield and horse racing, from using the horse's flute, and from guild gathering orders and creating a guild fort.

To leave, either finish the quest from the NPC at the jail entrance, which removes the debuff, or slip past the guard NPC to the exit. A quest already in progress blocks escape even without the debuff, so complete or abandon it first. Afterward, karma 0 or higher sends you to Ibellab Oasis, and -1 or lower sends you to the bandit haven of Muiquun.

## Death penalties: negative naval fame and pirates

Naval fame rises by killing sea monsters and falls by attacking or destroying other Adventurers' ships. If it drops below 0, you become a pirate and coastal guard NPCs at each town's wharf attack you. If another character or NPC kills you, magic crystals and lightstones may be destroyed, and an item's enhancement level may decrease.

Most wharf managers cannot be used while you are a pirate. Ship supplies can only be bought from the wharf manager of the Kuit Islands, Gapur.
