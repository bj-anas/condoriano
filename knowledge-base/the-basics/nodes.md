# Nodes

The node system divides the world into regions you connect with contribution points. This page covers node types, investing, connecting, production nodes, and withdrawing.

## Nodes overview and benefits

Nodes are central to trading and life skills. The world is divided into nodes that each Adventurer discovers, and connected nodes allow trading across distant lands without penalty, sending workers to gather resources, and a higher item drop rate for valuable loot from monsters. Connecting nodes requires investing contribution points. Connected towns pay less silver for item transport, while unconnected towns triple it. You can sell trade goods to trade managers anywhere, but if the nodes between where you got the good and where you sell it are not connected, the sale price is reduced to 30% of its value. For workshop crafting, a worker from town A can use town A's storage to craft in town B only if both towns are connected; otherwise "Not Connected" shows in red after the worker's town.

## Nodes: exploration node types

Nodes are split into exploration nodes and production nodes. Exploration nodes are the first type you see on the world map and fall into six categories:

- City: urban centers with trade, storage, housing, transport, and more, such as Velia and Heidel. They can serve as base nodes on discovery, and the leader (a Chief or Lord) shows the Lordship Menu.
- Town: most merchants but fewer services (some lack storage). Some activate by default, while others such as Florin need an investment.
- Trade: trading, housing, and node production options may be available.
- Gateway: frontier areas near a border or hostile area, with trade at certain camps and frequent monster night raids.
- Danger Zone: dangerous areas surrounded by monsters; affiliated production nodes may be usable.
- Node: usually a lone node manager NPC or an object replacing one; production options may be available.

Cities and towns are always safe zones; the others are usually combat zones.

## Nodes: investing and discovery

Networks grow from the main cities and towns, for instance Velia, Heidel, and Glish. Every city node unlocks by itself, many town nodes do too, and the remaining nodes must be activated with contribution investment. Every node has a node manager NPC, or sometimes an object, with the node management interface. To invest you must have discovered the node and have it connected to an active node. Without discovery, the Node Management button does not show. Discover a node by exploring until you get its knowledge entry, or by interacting with its node manager, who may ask for energy. A white line between two nodes means the adjacent node can be invested in and connected now; connected nodes show orange.

## Nodes: connecting with Previous Node

If you know where a node is but not how to connect it, click it and use the Previous Node button on the left side. Light pillars mark all nodes you could invest in to connect to the selected one; invested nodes are not shown. If you have not discovered a nearby node, its pillar marks where to discover the area, not the manager's location. For example, to activate the Western Guard Camp, select it, press Previous Node, and trace a path to an already connected node. If a connected node has no white line leading on, explore nearby for the next adjacent node.

## Nodes: connecting example, Velia to Heidel

To connect Velia and Heidel: go to Emma Bartali, the node manager of the Bartali Farm node, which has a white line to Velia. Press Node Management (hotkey: 3) and then Invest Contribution. Next, Balenos Forest connects to it; its manager is Daphne DelLucci at DelLucci Farm. Then walk south along the road from Velia to find the border checkpoint, which is the Heidel Pass node. Finally, the Northern Guard Camp north of Heidel completes the link. Node managers usually have a flag object nearby. Connected nodes let you buy and sell trade goods along the route at full price; unconnected nodes give a 30% price penalty.

## Nodes: production nodes and workers

A production node is a satellite node (subnode) of an exploration node. There are 5 types: Farming & Gathering, Mining, Lumbering, Production, and Excavation. After investing contribution, you can send hired workers to gather resources, for example potatoes from a potato farm or chicken meat from a chicken production node; one example work cycle takes around 40 minutes. Hire workers through contracts with a work supervisor in town; each town allows 1 worker at first, and more require buying houses for worker lodging. Gathered items go to the storage of the worker's own town, so a Velia worker gathering fleece in Serendia delivers it to Velia's storage. A full storage stops the worker. At Lv. 40 a worker can choose which town storage receives the items. Lucky workers may bring extra items, such as a farmer's sack that yields a fruit for alchemy. See the Workers page.

## Nodes: retrieving investment

To withdraw, open the World Map, click an invested node, and press Withdraw Contribution on the left. A node in the middle of a network, tied to two or more nodes, cannot be disconnected, and the button does not appear. Start from the nearest end of the network and disconnect inward. For instance, Forest of Plunder cannot be withdrawn until the Goblin Cave node at the end is disconnected. If production nodes are invested in the node, withdraw those first. If a worker is on the node, wait for them to finish or cancel their activity.

## Nodes: saving contribution when connecting

On the world map, a route icon shows the most contribution-efficient way to connect unlocked nodes. Under Connect Node Directly, enter the start and end node names in the search window or click them, then press Preview to highlight the best route. With an active value pack you can connect nodes remotely without visiting the node managers, at a cost of 10 energy per node.
